Adds To Cash Now

Cash-in entries increase Net Cash Flow right away.

This includes normal income you add manually, imported income, and Other Cash In such as fiat crypto sell proceeds, lent repayments, refunds, reimbursements, asset sales, own-account cash transfers, and debt increases only when you explicitly mark that borrowing as cash received on a date.

Takes Cash Away Now

Cash spending, credit card payments, and BNPL payments reduce Net Cash Flow immediately.

This covers regular expenses, bill payments paid by you, wishlist conversions once saved, reminder quick-adds saved as cash expenses, crypto buy expense mirrors, CSV or bridge expenses, debt repayments, credit card payment records, and installment/BNPL payments. Card and BNPL payments are cash leaving you now, even though they settle debt instead of creating a new purchase.

Recorded, Not Cash Gone Yet

Credit-card charges count as Spending when purchased, but they do not lower Net Cash Flow until you record the card payment.

Debt increase entries belong here when they are only tracking that your liability grew. If the borrowing actually gave you money in hand then, use the borrowed-date option so that debt increase also adds to cash on that date.

Where These Movements Come From

Recent Movements includes transactions saved directly from Add Transaction, Non-Income Cash In entries, Reminders quick-add, Credit Card Payment, BNPL Payment, Electricity bill payment records, Debt activity, Wishlist conversion, CSV Import, bridge imports from the main app, and auto-created crypto buy/sell mirror entries. Bills, debts, credit cards, BNPL plans, and wishlist items only appear here after they create an actual transaction.

Quick rule: Spending records consumption when it happens; Net Cash Flow records when cash actually moves.
